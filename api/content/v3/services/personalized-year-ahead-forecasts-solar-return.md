## Classical Vedic Methodology & Texts Used

### Varshaphala: the Tajika annual chart
The Tajika system is set out in **Tajika Neelakanthi**, the classical work by Neelakantha. Each year, the moment the Sun returns to its exact natal longitude in the sidereal zodiac produces a new chart, the **Varsha Pravesha**, for your current location. From it I read:

- **The Varsha Lagna:** the ascendant of the year and the tone it sets.
- **Muntha:** a sensitive point that advances one sign each year from your natal Lagna. The house it occupies in the annual chart shows a major theme of the year.
- **The Varsheshwara (lord of the year):** chosen from the five office-bearers, the Panchadhikaris, according to Tajika rules. Its strength colours the whole year.
- **Tajika yogas and aspects:** Ithasala (applying), Ishrafa (separating) and the other classical yogas, which describe whether matters come together or slip away.
- **Sahams:** sensitive points such as Punya (fortune), Vivaha (marriage) and Karma (work), used for specific questions.
- **Mudda dasha:** a dasha compressed into a single year, which divides your twelve months into planetary periods.

I always read the annual chart **against your birth chart and your running Vimshottari dasha**. A year can only deliver what the natal promise and the dasha allow.

### Prashna: horary questions
**Prashna Shastra** casts a chart for the moment a sincere question is asked. I examine the Prashna Lagna and its lord, the Moon, the house of the question and its lord, and the Tajika aspects between them, following the Prashna section of Tajika Neelakanthi. Prashna suits focused, time-sensitive questions: "Will this job offer come through?", "Is this the right property?", "Will the visa be approved this year?"

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Varshaphala the same as a Western solar return?
Both use the Sun's return to its birth position, but Varshaphala uses the sidereal zodiac and the Tajika system: Muntha, the year lord, Tajika aspects, Sahams and Mudda dasha. It is also always read together with your Vimshottari dasha, which makes it a distinctly Vedic annual forecast.

### When should I book a Varshaphala reading?
The best time is a few weeks before your birthday, when the new annual chart is about to begin. Booking around the new year also works well. If your birthday has recently passed, the current year's chart is still fully valid, and I read it from today forward.

### Can Prashna answer my question if I do not know my birth time?
Yes. Prashna uses a chart cast for the moment you ask your sincere question, so a birth time is not required. It works best for one clear, specific question asked once, such as a job offer, a property, travel or a relationship decision.
